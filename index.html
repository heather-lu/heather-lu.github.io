Predicting COVID-19 illness severity

Spring 2026, Columbia University, in R

The complex models barely beat a simple one. AdaBoost reached a test AUC of 0.918, while logistic regression reached 0.911.

I compared logistic regression, a decision tree, AdaBoost, and a radial-kernel SVM for classifying severe COVID-19 in 1,000 participants, tuning each with 10-fold cross-validation. I turned the final model into an individual risk score. Variable importance and partial dependence plots showed that vaccination status was the strongest predictor. Risk rose sharply once systolic blood pressure passed about 130 mmHg and BMI passed 25.

Breast cancer progression-free survival across cohorts

Spring 2026, Columbia University, in R, with Veerapetch Petchger and Clement Li

A model trained on an earlier cohort lost accuracy on a later one. Its prediction error rose by about 21%.

We compared six models for log-transformed progression-free survival: OLS, ridge, lasso, elastic net, GAM, and MARS. We used 10-fold cross-validation in a cohort of 2,000 patients. Cross-validation errors were close, so we chose lasso for parsimony and interpretability. We then tested it on a later cohort of 2,000. Along with the drop in accuracy, coefficients for HER2 status, hormonal therapy, and lymph nodes shifted between the two periods.

Code and report on GitHub

Breast cancer outcomes and protein biomarkers

Fall 2025, Columbia University, in R, group project for Data Science I

Do clinical characteristics and four protein biomarkers predict vital status in 334 surgically treated breast cancer patients? I fit multivariable logistic regression and checked discrimination with ROC/AUC across five Monte Carlo train/test splits. I also estimated Kaplan–Meier curves by tumor stage and HER2 status for 317 eligible patients. The evidence was limited, and I report it that way: the cohort was small, there were few deaths, and the subgroups were uneven.
